Privacy

Updated November 25, 2026. This page describes the app as it works today.

What stays on your device

Your gratitude entries, photos, voice recordings and tags, the time each entry was written and how long it took, your emoji garden, your recipes, your wishes and your name are stored in your browser on your device. If you clear the browser's site data, or use “Start over” in the app, they are gone. “Export everything” saves a copy as a file you keep. Voice recordings are not in that file.

What travels when you choose

A Gratus Gift is packed inside the link you share. The link carries the emoji, the days it took, the phases it passed through and the dates, one short line from an entry on each of those dates, the count of your entries about that emoji, your tags, the message you wrote, who it is for, and the name you signed. Anyone who has the link can open it. Choose what you put in a gift with that in mind. The person who receives it can tell you that it arrived or that it grew. That message carries a phase, the emoji and the day, with no name and no words.

A goal you post goes to a public stream: one line, the name you gave, an emoji and the time. A word you post in a Vibe is kept on our server and can be read by anyone who holds that room's code. A Gratus Seed is a note written to a Giveth project you gave to. The people who run that project can read it, and it is public only if you publish it.

A voice entry sends the recording once to ElevenLabs to be turned into words. The recording is not kept on our server. It stays on your device.

A PAXT you open or join is kept on our server, and anyone with its link can read it: the text, and for each key that joined, the line it signed, its signature, the public half of the key, the name chosen (if any) and the time. The private half of each key is made on your device and never leaves it, so it is lost if you clear the browser's site data or use “Start over”. Each PAXT gets its own key, so two PAXTs cannot be linked by key. To stop one person filling a PAXT with keys, the server also keeps a count of how many keys each network added to it, under a salted hash with no address and no key in it. The count is deleted the first time the PAXT is opened after it closes. That count grows by one in the same moment a new key's first line is kept. Until the count is deleted, someone holding our store could match those times and tell which keys and names in that PAXT came from one network, on any day it was open. Each time a PAXT is opened or signed, the server also keeps a marker for that day's limits, filed under a salted hash of the network that changes every day. By matching a marker's time to a signed line, someone holding our store could tell which keys and names came from one network that day, and someone holding both our store and our secret could work out the network. Markers from earlier days are deleted with the first call of each new day.

A signal asked in a Vibe is kept on our server: the question, the choices and when it opens and closes. Anyone who holds that room's code can read it. When you answer, your browser makes a token from a secret kept on your device and the signal's id, so your device gives a different token for every signal. The server keeps your choice under a name made from that token. When your device reads a room, it sends its tokens for that room together, and they are used for that read and not kept. Each answer also leaves a marker for that day's limits, filed under a salted hash of your network, and one network's markers for a day share a name. By matching the markers' times to the answers, someone holding our store could tell which answers that day came from one network, and someone holding both our store and our secret could work out the network. Markers from earlier days are deleted with the first call of each new day. The server keeps no name or address for anyone who answered, and the app shows nobody, including the person who asked, who chose what. While a signal is open, nobody sees its counts or how many devices have answered; your device sees only its own choice. The counts show to everyone only when the signal closes, and then the totals are kept. In a small group the final counts can sometimes show how someone chose: when two people answer, each can work out the other's choice. A signal holds 500 to 749 answers. Only the server knows the exact number, which is different for each signal, so someone who fills an open signal with answers of their own can tell little about how many had answered before them. The daily limits on asking, answering and reading signals count each connection by that same salted hash. A room's list of PAXTs keeps each PAXT's id and the time it was added, and anyone who holds the room's code can read the ids. Each try at adding a PAXT to a room leaves a marker for that day's limit under a salted hash of the network, and markers from earlier days are deleted with the first call of each new day.

Accounts

An account is optional. It keeps a copy of your garden that is encrypted on your device with a key made from your password, so we cannot read it. For the same reason we cannot reset a password. We keep a salted hash of your email and never the email itself. If you publish a page, it shows your handle, your name, a line you wrote and the shape of your garden. It never shows an entry. The list of people you keep is stored with your account.

Paying

If you subscribe, Stripe takes the payment on its own page and no card number reaches our server. We keep whether your subscription is active.

What we count

We count visits, the seconds a page was open, gifts wrapped, gifts planted, seeds planted and days of care, as totals. A device is counted once a day by a salted hash that is never an address. The limits on voice entries, new rooms, sign-in attempts, signals asked, answered and read, and PAXTs added to rooms use the same kind of hash. So do the daily limits on opening and signing a PAXT. There are no ads and no third-party trackers.

Other services

Fonts load from Google Fonts, which sees your browser's request for the font files. The site and its storage run on Vercel. Pages about Giveth read Giveth's public API, and a public wallet address you paste for Sunlight is sent there to be read. Links to partners open their sites, which have their own policies.
