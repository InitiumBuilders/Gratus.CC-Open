title: A PAXT, a promise you sign together

A PAXT is one plain text that a group joins by each signing one line. The line names the
text by its fingerprint, so a signature can only ever mean the exact words that were read.
When enough have joined, it is in force. Anyone with the link can check every signature and
count again.

**Writing one.** Open Gratus Gives Together, then Gratus PAXTs, and press Write a PAXT. Give
it a name, write what you are promising each other, and choose how many must join and the
day it closes. A new PAXT starts with an intent line: a promise kept by choice, not a
contract, and it moves no money. Keep that line, so nobody joins without meaning to. Once a
PAXT is open, its text cannot change.

**Joining.** Open the link and read the text. The clerk reads it with you: it numbers the
sentences, and it marks anything worth a second look, such as an amount of money, a link to
another site, or words asking for a recovery phrase. Press Join, choose the name others will
see or leave it empty, and your device signs one line. Nothing is signed until you press
Join.

**Your key.** Your device makes a key for this PAXT alone and keeps it. The key never leaves
the device, and a key made for one PAXT is never used for another, so two PAXTs cannot be
tied together by key. If you clear the browser's site data or press Start over, the key goes
with it, and that device can no longer leave or change its choice. No export keeps it.
Safari can also clear a site's storage after seven days of use without a visit, while Gratus
on your Home Screen keeps its own. The PAXT page says whether this device still holds its
key.

**Counting.** Any key can join, and one person can hold many keys, so every count is a count
of keys, and the page says so. Nothing ever lists who has not joined.

**Leaving.** Press Leave while the PAXT is open. Your device signs one more line, and the
newer line is the one counted. A leave line carries no name, but it is signed with the same key
as your join, so anyone reading the record can tell that the key that joined, and the name it
joined under, has left.

**Checking it yourself.** Every PAXT shows its fingerprint in groups of four, so two people
can read it to each other and know they are looking at the same text. A matching
fingerprint means the same text, and nothing more. Recount it yourself saves the text and
every signed line as a file that anyone can count again, on any device.

**A PAXT room.** A Vibe room can hold the PAXTs its people write together. The room shows how
many keys have joined each one. Signed lines stay on each PAXT's own page.

**A signal.** In a room, Ask the room puts one quick question with a few choices. It binds
no one. Each device answers once and can change its answer until the question closes. While
it is open, nobody sees the counts, and you see only your own answer. The counts show to
everyone only when it closes. Nobody, including the person who asked, is shown who chose what,
though in a small group the final counts can sometimes show how someone chose.

**What anyone with the link can read.** The text, the count, every signed line, the names
people chose and the time each line arrived. Your journal is never part of a PAXT.
